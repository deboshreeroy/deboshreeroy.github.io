Spotify Listening Retention & Engagement

A product analytics case study using Spotify listening data to understand retention patterns, engagement segments, and actionable drivers of long-term usage.

product analytics cohorts retention segmentation
← back to selected work

Overview

What I did, what I found, and what I’d recommend if this were a real product team.

Problem

Retention is often a story told in averages. I wanted to understand how listening behavior varies across users: who sticks, who churns, and what behaviors predict long-term engagement.

  • How does retention vary by first-week behavior?
  • Which engagement patterns correlate with long-term usage?
  • What segments should a product team prioritize?

Data

Spotify listening history export (personal data) or a synthetic dataset that mirrors real listening event structure. Events include timestamps, track/artist metadata, and session-level behavior.

  • Unit of analysis: user-week & sessions
  • Key metrics: sessions/week, minutes listened, diversity, repeats
  • Outputs: cohort curves + segment profiles
Figure: Cohort retention curve (placeholder — add your plot screenshot here).

Method

Built a retention view (cohorts by first-week start date), then layered segmentation and predictor analysis:

  • Cohort retention curves (weekly active listeners)
  • Sessionization (gap-based) for session frequency and depth
  • Engagement segments (power / regular / casual)
  • Churn proxy model (e.g., no activity in N weeks)

Key insights

This section becomes your “product memo.” Keep it crisp: 3 findings + 3 recommendations.

  • Example finding: early habit formation predicts long-term retention
  • Example finding: session frequency matters more than session length
  • Example finding: diversity vs repeats indicates different listener intents

Recommendations

  • Personalize onboarding based on first-week behavior signals
  • Build nudges for “near-regular” users at risk of drop-off
  • Experiment with playlist prompts that increase weekly habit

What I’d do next

  • Define experiment candidates + success metrics
  • Test segmentation stability over time
  • Add causal framing (e.g., propensity adjustment) for interventions
