Reading & Thinking

Book

Klara and the Sun

Kazuo Ishiguro

The idea that sunlight is literally nourishment, that it powers Klara's capacity to understand and feel, is such an elegant metaphor for how experience shapes us.

Underneath that is a deeper question: what actually makes us us? Klara watches humans with such careful, generous attention, more than most humans watch each other, and that irony sits at the heart of the novel.

The most honest fictional portrait of humanist intelligence I've encountered.

Book

Thinking, Fast and Slow

Daniel Kahneman

This book taught me to slow down and be more deliberate, to catch when I'm pattern-matching too quickly and mistaking confidence for correctness.

But it also changed how I think about intuition. System 1 isn't the enemy; it's information. The emotional pull toward something is worth examining, not suppressing.

The biases in our data are the biases in our cognition, just made legible, and an insight is only as good as the mental model of the person receiving it.

Paper

On the Measure of Intelligence

François Chollet

Chollet's skepticism about benchmarks captures something I think about constantly: models can pass tests but fail at novel problems.

It raises a deeper question: are we measuring intelligence, or memorization? The idea that general intelligence is about adaptation, not pattern recall, reframes what it means to build something genuinely capable.

Book

The Alignment Problem

Brian Christian

What draws me to this is the same thread running through Klara: what does it mean to build something oriented toward people, not just toward a task?

Christian asks whether AI does what we actually want, not just what we asked for, and that gap between instruction and intent is where the most interesting, and most dangerous, territory lives.
