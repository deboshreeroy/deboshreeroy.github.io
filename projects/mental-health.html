Predicting Mental Health
from Wearable Data.

A causal-inference study using biometric data from 17,500+ participants in the NIH All of Us dataset to identify behavioral drivers of mental health.

Role
MS Thesis · Sole researcher
Stack
Python · scikit-learn · LiNGAM
Year
2024
Hero figure — pipeline diagram
The question

What can wearable data tell us about mental health, before it shows up in a survey?

[Placeholder — describe the gap: most mental health prediction relies on self-report. Wearables capture behavior continuously, but most analyses are correlational. Can we move from passive tracking to actionable early insight?]

Approach

From raw signal to causal claim, in four stages.

[Placeholder — walk through the pipeline: pre-processing biometric streams, K-Means clustering on behavioral features, regression to depression outcomes, LiNGAM to test directionality.]

What I found

Sleep and steps — not heart rate — drive the signal.

[Placeholder — call out the key finding: sleep duration and daily step count emerged as the strongest behavioral predictors. LiNGAM revealed direct (not just correlational) paths to depression risk.]
